# Customer-Discovery Interview Guide

**Goal of this phase:** Talk to 5–10 working forensic experts BEFORE building anything. We are testing whether the pain, the willingness to pay, and the AI-disclosure moat thesis are real — and choosing the single beachhead discipline. The plan's #1 lesson: unchecked ideas look better than they are.

**Who to talk to:** Practicing forensic engineers (ASTM E3176 world) and accident reconstructionists (IPTM/ACTAR world). Solo or small-firm preferred — they feel report-writing pain personally and buy self-serve. (Avoid medical/IME experts for now: PHI/HIPAA and a crowded field.)

**Format:** 25–30 min call. Learn, do not pitch. Talk <30% of the time. Never lead the witness. If you mention the product at all, do it only in the last 5 minutes.

**The 5 things this phase must resolve** (from the plan's validation checklist):
1. Real report-writing time/pain + willingness to pay.
2. Which discipline to beachhead (engineering vs reconstruction).
3. Exact template/format expectations (ASTM E3176 vs IPTM/ACTAR specifics).
4. Whether the AI-disclosure/audit appendix is perceived as valuable (the moat).
5. Report-volume per year (settles per-report vs. seat pricing).
6. Convert one credible interviewee into a paid design partner/advisor.

## Warm-up (2 min)
- "Thanks for the time. I'm researching how forensic experts produce their written reports — not selling anything, just trying to understand the workflow from people who actually do it. Mind if I ask how you got into expert work?"
- "Roughly how many reports do you write in a typical year?" *(→ Q5: volume)*
- "Solo, or part of a firm? Who does the writing — you, or staff?"

## The workflow & the pain (10 min) — the core
Anchor on their **most recent** report (past-behavior beats hypotheticals):
- "Walk me through the last report you wrote, start to finish. What did you start with?"
- "What were the raw inputs?" *(inspection notes, scene photos, measurements, calcs, depositions, police/incident reports, the engagement letter — note which are messy vs structured → informs intake design, Must-Have #3)*
- "How long did the writing itself take, separate from the investigation?" *(→ Q1: pain magnitude)*
- "Do you bill that writing time, write it off, or eat it?" *(if they write it off, the pain is felt as lost personal time — strong signal)*
- "What part of writing it is the most tedious or soul-crushing?"
- "What format/template did you follow? Your own? A firm template? ASTM E3176 / the IPTM topical format / an ACTAR overlay?" *(→ Q3: template specifics — get them to send a redacted sample if willing)*
- "Last time you got a report out the door late or sloppy — what caused it?"

## Tools today (4 min)
- "What do you use to write — Word, a template, anything else?"
- "Have you tried any AI tools (Copilot, ChatGPT) for any of it? What happened?" *(listen for the rebuke/fear — feeds the moat)*
- "If a junior could draft the boilerplate sections from your notes and findings, leaving you to write the opinions — would that help, or is the boilerplate not the bottleneck?"

## The AI-disclosure / admissibility angle (5 min) — moat test
Do NOT explain our product first. Probe the pain:
- "Has opposing counsel ever challenged your methodology or how you prepared the report?"
- "Are you aware of the recent rulings where courts ordered experts to disclose the AI prompts they used in a report?" *(CLF v. Shell, 2026)*
- "If you used an AI assistant to help draft, how nervous would you be about disclosure or a Daubert challenge?"
- "Would a tool that automatically produced a court-ready record of exactly what it did — every source, every step, nothing invented — make you more or less likely to use AI?" *(→ Q4: is the audit trail valuable, or do they not care?)*
- **Kill-criterion to listen for:** if they shrug at disclosure AND say boilerplate isn't their bottleneck, this discipline/segment is weak — note it.

## Willingness to pay (3 min)
- "If a tool reliably saved you [X hours they quoted] per report, what would that be worth to you per report?"
- "Would you rather pay per report, or a flat monthly subscription?" *(→ Q5 pricing model)*
- "What would have to be true for you to trust it with a report that goes out under your signature?" *(captures the liability bar, Must-Have #1)*

## Close + design-partner ask (2 min) — Must-Have #2
- "This has been incredibly helpful. I'm building exactly this for [their discipline], and I'm looking for 1–2 experts to design it WITH me — review drafts, make sure it's truly compliant, in exchange for free lifetime access and a real say in the product (and I pay for your time on calls). Would you be open to that, or know someone who'd be perfect?"
- "Anyone else in [discipline] I should talk to?" *(referral chain — these communities run on referrals)*

## Decision gates after 5–10 calls
- **GO + which discipline:** the one with (a) more acute writing pain, (b) clearer template standard, (c) real disclosure anxiety, (d) ≥1 willing design partner.
- **Pricing:** if median volume is low (<~10 reports/yr), lead with per-report credits, not seats.
- **NO-GO signal:** if most say boilerplate isn't the bottleneck and disclosure doesn't worry them — pause and reconsider before building.
