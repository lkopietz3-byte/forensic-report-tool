# Outreach & Sourcing Guide

How to find 5–10 forensic experts to interview and convert one into a paid design partner (plan Must-Have #2). These are conservative, referral-driven communities — warm and specific beats cold and generic.

## Where to find them

**Forensic engineering**
- **NAFE** (National Academy of Forensic Engineers) — member directory; the core community.
- **NSPE** forensic-engineering groups and state PE societies.
- **ASTM E58** (forensic engineering committee) participants — authors of the E3176 standard you'd template against.
- **ForensisGroup**, **Robson Forensic**, **Rimkus**, **Engineering Systems Inc.** — referral firms/large shops (good for understanding the workflow; solos are easier first customers).

**Accident reconstruction**
- **ACTAR** (Accreditation Commission for Traffic Accident Reconstruction) — accredited-reconstructionist directory.
- **IPTM** (Institute of Police Technology and Management) course alumni networks — IPTM defines the de-facto report format.
- **NAPARS** (National Association of Professional Accident Reconstruction Specialists).
- **SAE** crash-reconstruction community.

**Cross-discipline**
- **SEAK** — the expert-witness training/directory org; runs conferences and publishes the fee surveys. Best single hub for reaching experts across disciplines and for eventual distribution.
- **JurisPro**, **Expert Institute**, **Round Table Group** — expert directories; good for finding named solos.
- **LinkedIn** — search titles: "forensic engineer", "accident reconstructionist", "PE forensic", "ACTAR".

## Sourcing tactics (fastest → slowest)
1. **Warm intros first.** Anyone in your network connected to litigation, insurance, PE/engineering, or law? One warm intro outperforms 50 cold messages in a referral community.
2. **Referral chaining.** End every interview with "who else should I talk to?" — engineered into the interview guide. This is how you actually get to 10.
3. **Directory + personalized LinkedIn.** Pull 30 named solos from ACTAR/NAFE/JurisPro, send tailored notes (below).
4. **Conferences.** SEAK and discipline conferences = dozens of target experts in one room. Worth attending one early for credibility + design-partner recruiting.

## Outreach templates

### A. Cold LinkedIn / email — interview request (no pitch)
> Subject: 25 min on how forensic [engineers / reconstructionists] write reports?
>
> Hi [Name] — I'm researching the report-writing workflow of forensic [discipline] experts (not selling anything). You came up via [ACTAR directory / NAFE / a mutual connection / your work on X].
>
> I'm trying to understand, from people who actually do it, where the time really goes when turning an investigation into a written report. Would you be open to a 25-minute call in the next couple weeks? Happy to share what I learn across the interviews in return.
>
> Thanks either way — [You]

Keep it short, specific to them, zero product talk. Reply rate lives or dies on the "(not selling anything)" being true.

### B. Referral follow-up (after an interviewee offers a name)
> Hi [Name] — [Referrer] suggested I reach out. I'm talking with forensic [discipline] experts about how they produce written reports (research, not a sales call). [Referrer] thought you'd have a sharp perspective given [specifics]. Open to a 25-min call?

### C. Design-partner ask (after a strong interview — Must-Have #2)
> [Name], our conversation was exactly the kind of insight I need. I'm building a tool that drafts court-ready [discipline] reports from the expert's own findings — structuring and formatting, never inventing facts or opinions — with an automatic AI-disclosure record built for Rule 26 / Daubert.
>
> I'm looking for one or two experts to build it WITH me as design partners: you review drafts and tell me where it's wrong on compliance and substance; in exchange you get free lifetime access, real influence over the roadmap, and I pay for your time on our working calls. Maybe 2–3 hours/month. Would you be up for it?

## Guardrails (so you don't burn the community)
- **Never imply the tool writes opinions.** In a referral network, one "it'll fabricate your testimony" impression spreads and is fatal (Must-Have #1). Always frame: *structures and formats the expert's own findings; the expert authors and signs.*
- **Lead with research, not sales.** Trust is the scarce resource here.
- **Confidentiality up front.** Many will mention protective orders. Have your answer ready: no training on their data (Anthropic API default), encrypted, written confidentiality terms (Must-Have #6).
- **Track everyone** in `discovery/contacts.csv` (name, discipline, source, status, last touch, referrals) so the referral chain compounds.
