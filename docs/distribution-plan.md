# Distribution Plan — Disclosed. (research-backed, 2026-06-19)

The execution-level distribution plan, built from deep research whose claims were
adversarially fact-checked (sources at the bottom). Complements `marketing-plan.md`
(positioning, messaging, claims hygiene) — this doc is **channels + 90-day actions**.

> **Two hard rules, from the research itself:**
> 1. **Deploy is Step 0.** None of this has a surface to point at until the site is
>    live. (See `DEPLOY.md`.)
> 2. **The metric is ONE paid design partner — not waitlist size.** A real B2B-AI
>    founder got *500 signups in 30 days and 0 paying customers* [IH]. Vanity
>    signups are a trap; optimize for revenue and a named champion.

---

## 1. Positioning the research validated (use THEIR words, never "AI")

- The credibility-killer for an expert is AI **originating the opinion**, not AI
  assisting. The explicit, career-ending line is "AI generated the opinion";
  "AI helped me structure my own findings" is on the safe side [KevinMD; J.S. Held].
  → **Lead with human authorship + accountability.** Never sell AI capability.
- SEAK's own Daubert guidance (Babitsky, updated **Feb 2026**) says experts must show
  findings can be "replicated," state a "known error rate," and keep documented
  "standards and controls" [SEAK]. → Sell the disclosure/audit trail as
  **"methodology documentation,"** the exact language they already trust.
- Experts are coached to avoid anything "novel or litigation-driven" [SEAK]. → Frame
  the product as disclosure/methodology **discipline**, not a shiny AI tool.

## 2. PRIMARY STRATEGY — the two-sided / demand-side wedge (biggest finding)

Don't only push the tool at the tiny, slow pool of experts. **Create pull from the
retaining attorneys**, where the demand is already being manufactured:

- Courts now treat expert AI use as discoverable Rule-26 methodology [Mayer Brown;
  Arnold & Porter].
- **The unlock:** firms are advising retaining attorneys to **add a contractual
  provision requiring experts to disclose how AI was used** [Harris Beach Murtha],
  and that AI disclosure "matters more for expert reports than legal briefs."
- Precedent for "AI-disclosure-ready as best practice": **J.S. Held amended its
  standard engagement letter** to disclose AI-by-default to clients [J.S. Held].

**What we build/do for this side:**
- Sharpen `/for-counsel` into a demand-generation asset (currently an explainer).
- An attorney one-pager + LinkedIn angle: *"Three ways your expert's AI use gets
  them excluded under Rule 702 — and the disclosure that prevents it."*
- Position **`/verify`** as free public infrastructure attorneys (and opposing
  counsel) can use → spreads the standard + the brand with zero sales pressure.

## 4. What's DEAD here — do not spend on it (evidence-backed)

- **Generic SEO** — the zero-click / AI-Overview organic crisis [Digital Bloom].
- **Paid search/social ads** [Search Engine Land] and **cold mass email**
  [Built for B2B] — wrong register for a trust-driven niche; Siege Media even
  **stopped manual link-building after 1M+ emails**.
- **Chasing waitlist/signup counts** — see the "500 signups, 0 customers" tax [IH].
- **Product Hunt / tech-launch theater** — wrong audience entirely.

## 5. The compounding engine (cheap, AI-buildable, owned)

- **"State of AI in Expert Reports 2026" data study** — survey the community +
  analyze the public rulings. The data-study → digital-PR → **get-cited-by-LLMs**
  loop is a documented, repeatable play [Siege Media]. Becomes an annual franchise.
- **A niche newsletter** (Beehiiv) — short factual roundup of AI-in-litigation +
  expert-disclosure developments. The owned audience that compounds.
- **GEO** — make `/resources/ai-disclosure-in-expert-reports` and
  `/resources/rule-26-expert-report-checklist` the canonical, dated, structured
  answer engines cite when anyone asks "how do I disclose AI use in a Rule 26 report."
- **Free embeddable tools** — `/verify`, a Rule-26 completeness checklist — as
  word-of-mouth wedges.

## 6. 90-day plan (solo, ~$300–600/mo, mostly time)

- **Weeks 1–2 — Foundation.** Deploy. Lock **vocational rehab** as the beachhead.
  Stand up the Beehiiv newsletter; publish `/verify` as a public standard; GEO-pass
  the two resource pages; finalize the design-partner shortlist (ABVE/IARP + LinkedIn).
- **Weeks 3–6 — Design partner + demand seeds.** 10–20 personalized design-partner
  outreaches. Launch the data-study survey (Tally). Newsletter weekly. Pitch
  "Engaging Experts" as a guest. First attorney-side LinkedIn post (the Rule-702 angle).
- **Weeks 7–10 — Convert + amplify.** Sign 1–2 design partners; co-build the RAPEL
  template; red-team one real report. Run a free CLE-style webinar co-hosted with the
  partner (experts + attorneys in one room). Publish Data Study v1 → digital-PR push.
- **Weeks 11–12 — First paying cohort.** 3–5 beta experts each produce a real report;
  convert via the referral chain; measure the referral coefficient; plan one
  ABVE/IARP appearance.

**Buy:** Beehiiv (~$0–40/mo), Tally (free), Zoom (~$15/mo), one event sponsorship
*later* (~$800 amortized, the one real spend). **Build:** the `/verify` public page,
the data-study microsite + an embeddable checklist widget, GEO resource pages.

## 7. First 10 customers — cold-start sequence

1. **Design partner #1** (named; co-builds; endorses) — the keystone.
2. **Their warm referrals** (the chain is the engine).
3. **Beta cohort** (3–5 real reports) → testimonials + proof.
4. **One co-hosted CLE webinar** (experts + attorneys) → trust + leads.
5. **The data study's inbound** + one conference to compress the next 10.

## 8. Metrics that matter (and the ones to ignore)

- **Track:** # design-partner conversations, referrals generated per conversation,
  paid design partners (target ≥1), real reports produced end-to-end, referral
  coefficient.
- **Ignore:** waitlist size, raw signups, pageviews, social vanity counts.
