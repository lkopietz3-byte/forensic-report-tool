# Marketing & GTM Plan — Disclosed.

The one-document go-to-market plan. Synthesizes the discovery sourcing playbook
(`discovery/outreach-and-sourcing.md`), the market research
(`discovery/market-research.md`), and the copy/conversion/FTC audits
(`audit/09`, `audit/16`, `audit/18`). All messaging here obeys `docs/VOICE.md`
and the honesty rules in `CLAUDE.md`.

**Hard rule for everything below:** distribution, not build, is the bottleneck.
We are an unknown solo entering a conservative, referral-driven, slow-adopting
community. Budget for adoption slower than the build. Trust is the scarce
resource; we spend the whole plan buying it.

---

## 1. Positioning

**One line:** *Disclosed. structures a forensic expert's own findings into a
Rule 26(a)(2)(B)-organized report, with an automatic, tamper-evident
AI-Disclosure record built for the moment a court asks how you used AI.*

We are **a compliance-and-trust product that happens to structure reports** —
not a drafting wrapper. The draft generator is cloneable in ~3 months; the moat
is the disclosure/audit layer + one discipline's template quality + named-expert
endorsement.

**What we are NOT (say this plainly):**
- Not an opinion generator. The expert authors; the tool organizes.
- Not a guarantee of admissibility. That is always the court's call.
- Not a law firm; not legal advice.

---

## 2. Audience & beachhead

**Primary buyer:** solo and small-firm forensic expert witnesses who bill
$350–700/hr, write off report-drafting time, and fear cross-examination on AI
use.

**Beachhead discipline:** forensic **vocational rehabilitation /
earning-capacity** (RAPEL). Keep this fixed for the first 60–90 days of
interviews and pilots so the template, message, and referral loop can compound.
Accident reconstruction and forensic engineering remain the next two
template-additive candidates, not simultaneous launch markets. Do **not** go
horizontal; a "tool for all experts" dilutes template quality and kills the
trust advantage.

**Why these:** rigid templates to anchor quality, no PHI/HIPAA tax, reachable
self-serve populations, acute paid pain.

---

## 3. Messaging pillars (in priority order)

1. **You verify, prepare, adopt, and sign.** AI structures; you remain responsible. Every factual
   sentence traces to a source you supplied, or the tool flags the gap and asks
   you — it never fills it in. *(Defeats the #1 bounce: "AI-authored reports are
   unethical / cross-exam-fatal.")*
2. **The disclosure record courts are starting to ask for.** Append-only,
   tamper-evident audit log → a court-ready AI-Disclosure Appendix recording each
   AI-assisted section, the model/version, and the evidence it was given — and
   nothing it was not. *(The moat and the headline.)*
3. **Using AI is permitted; what's required is disclosure + ownership.** Lead
   with permission and the fix, not the threat. Reference ABA Formal Op. 512 and
   the 2026 discovery rulings factually, with the "not legal advice" caveat.
4. **Reclaim the drafting time you write off.** Framed as the target, attributed
   to the design cohort — never a bare guaranteed-hours stat.

---

## 4. Channels (warm & specific beats cold & generic)

Ordered fastest → slowest payoff:

1. **Warm intros + referral chaining.** One warm intro outperforms 50 cold
   messages in a referral community. End every interview/call with "who else
   should I talk to?" The referral chain is the whole distribution engine.
2. **Design-partner program.** 1–2 named experts per discipline who co-build the
   template, validate output, and lend referral credibility. This is
   simultaneously product, QA, and distribution. (Plan Must-Have #2.)
3. **Discipline communities / directories:** NAFE, NSPE, ASTM E58 (engineering);
   ACTAR, IPTM, NAPARS, SAE (reconstruction); SEAK as the cross-discipline hub
   and fee-survey publisher; JurisPro / Expert Institute / Round Table Group for
   named solos.
4. **Conferences:** SEAK and discipline conferences = dozens of target experts in
   one room. Attend one early for credibility + design-partner recruiting.
5. **Thought leadership / timing:** ride the *Conservation Law Foundation v.
   Shell* (2026) moment with factual, non-sensational writing on AI disclosure in
   expert reports. This is earned credibility, not paid ads — the right register
   for this audience.

Explicitly **not** doing pre-launch: paid ads, cold-blast email, broad PR. The
audience distrusts marketing gloss; spend on trust instead.

## 5. The conversion surface (landing page)

The pre-launch page's only jobs: **waitlist signups + design-partner
recruitment.** From the conversion audit (`audit/18`), in priority:

- **Hero leads with expert authorship + the ethics frame**, not the output. Make
  the expert the irrefutable author in the first few words. Promote the **sample
  report** to a distinct secondary CTA — a real output beats any copy for this
  audience.
- **Add a "Cross-Exam Ready" Q&A band** (the single highest-value addition):
  - "Did AI write your report?" → it's structured, not written by a computer; the
    appendix proves exactly how it was used.
  - "Are those citations real?" → closed-world grounding; every cite resolves to
    a source you supplied or it's flagged.
  - "Is this methodology / discoverable?" → yes, and the appendix is built for
    exactly that production.
- **Simplify pricing to 2 pre-launch options:** "Founding expert pilot" and
  "Target post-pilot price: $250/report." The pilot is free while only
  de-identified material is permitted. Once the confidentiality gate clears,
  the first production report is free and the list-price test begins. Hide firm
  tiers behind a "need multi-seat? talk to us" link.
- **Do not sell an unlimited annual plan yet.** The former $1,500 offer breaks
  even against the five-pack at only six reports while also promising founder
  support and template tuning. That bundles the highest-support customers into
  the lowest effective price before report volume or support cost is known.
- **Founder credibility:** 1–2 honest sentences on who built this and why. No
  fake bios; even minimal honest context beats silence.

All rewrites must pass `docs/VOICE.md` and `src/test/honesty.test.ts`.

## 6. Claims hygiene (FTC + ethics — non-negotiable)

From `audit/09`. These are launch-blocking if violated:

- **No data-security claim stated as present fact** unless the infrastructure and
  provider contract actually exist. Frame encryption / no-training / zero-
  retention / protective-order suitability as **commitments**, and publish a
  data-handling policy before accepting real case data.
- **Every case citation is real and attributed, or explicitly labeled
  illustrative.** This audience Googles cites for a living; a vague or fabricated
  precedent is fatal.
- **No "court-defensible / admissible / compliant" guarantees**; no absolute
  "can't / impossible" guarantees. Use the `VOICE.md` substitutions.
- **No unsubstantiated ROI / time-savings claims.** Attribute or reframe.
- **Sample is prominently marked fictional** (banner, not caption); the DOCX
  export already carries the SAMPLE notice.

---

## 7. Pricing thesis (validate before committing)

- Lead with **per-report credit pricing** ($250 single; test a $1,000 five-pack
  only after individual willingness-to-pay is established) because many experts
  write only a handful of reports/year, so monthly seat SaaS may not fit.
- Offer a capped annual bundle only after the report-volume distribution is
  known. Never label a variable-cost, high-support early plan "unlimited."
- Keep firm access as a design-partner conversation until shared templates,
  permissions, and pooled billing actually exist.
- **Validate report-volume distribution in discovery** before locking the model —
  $20k/mo MRR is harder than seat-math implies. (Validation question #5.)

## 8. Launch sequence

1. **Validation (now):** 5–10 discovery interviews → pick the discipline, settle
   pricing, confirm the disclosure moat resonates, convert ≥1 paid design
   partner. (See `discovery/interview-guide.md`.)
2. **Co-build the beachhead template** with the design partner; red-team one real
   report end-to-end against the discipline standard + Rule 26.
3. **Private beta:** 3–5 experts produce a real report each through intake →
   structure → disclosure appendix → DOCX export.
4. **Thought-leadership launch:** publish the AI-disclosure piece timed to the
   ruling; open the waitlist conversion path; recruit the next design-partner
   cohort by referral.
5. **Expand (Phase 2+):** same ingested case file → rebuttal reports, deposition
   prep, demonstratives, supplemental disclosures; then additional disciplines
   (template-additive). The disclosure layer scales uniformly.

---

## 9. Metrics that matter

- **Validation phase:** # interviews completed, # referrals generated per
  interview, # design partners signed (target ≥1 paid).
- **Beta:** # real reports produced end-to-end; # Rule 26 elements clean on first
  export; design-partner red-team pass.
- **Launch:** waitlist → activated report ratio; per-expert report volume
  (settles the pricing model); referral coefficient.
- **Do not** instrument vanity metrics. Define the event taxonomy before
  instrumenting (`DEFERRED.md`).

## 10. Risks & counters

| Risk | Counter |
| --- | --- |
| InspectMind / a funded incumbent adds Rule-26 narrative output | Go deeper on admissibility + AI-disclosure + one discipline's quality than a generalist will bother to; ship the disclosure layer as the headline, fast. |
| Generalist legal-AI (CaseMark etc.) bundles a shallow expert template | Discipline depth + named-expert endorsement + the audit trail they won't build. |
| Moat collapses if built as a pure drafting wrapper | The disclosure/audit layer is non-negotiable and is already in the MVP. |
| One "it fabricates testimony" impression spreads in the referral net | Never imply the tool writes opinions — in copy, outreach, or demos. Always: structures the expert's own findings; the expert authors and signs. |
| Slow adoption in a conservative community | This is expected, not a failure. Budget runway for it; lead with design-partner credibility and earned thought leadership, not ads. |

---

## 11. TAM honesty

The single-discipline beachhead realistically caps around low-single-digit-
millions ARR — a very good solo business, not a guaranteed unicorn. Upside
depends on the multi-discipline + adjacent-module expansion landing. Plan and
price for that expansion from the start; don't bank on it before the beachhead is
proven.
