## RAPEL in one line each (Weed — the dominant earning-capacity method)

| | Component | One line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R** | Rehabilitation plan | What it would realistically take to get this person back to work (retraining, cost, duration). |
| **A** | Access to labor market | How much of the labor market is still reachable — **home of the TSA.** |
| **P** | Placeability | Real-world likelihood of being *hired* — **where the labor-market survey is applied.** |
| **E** | Earning capacity | Pre- vs. post-injury income potential, **always a range, never a single number.** |
| **L** | Labor force participation | Realistic work consistency over the career + work-life-expectancy reduction. |

## The 6 Rule 26(a)(2)(B) report elements

1. **(i)** All **opinions** + the **basis and reasons** for them.
2. **(ii)** The **facts or data** the expert considered.
3. **(iii)** Any **exhibits** used to summarize or support the opinions.
4. **(iv)** **Qualifications**, including **publications (last 10 years).**
5. **(v)** All cases where the expert **testified** (trial/deposition) in the **last 4 years.**
6. **(vi)** Statement of **compensation** for the study and testimony.

> Disclosed. is **"Rule 26(a)(2)(B)-structured"** — organized to this format,
> tracks every element. **Never** say "compliant" or "guaranteed."

## The 5 AI-disclosure cases in one line each

| Case (court, date) | One line | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kohls v. Ellison** (D. Minn., Jan 10 2025) | Expert let GPT-4o **fill in citations**; it fabricated sources — "shatter[ed] his credibility." | Declaration **struck.** |
| **Concord Music Group v. Anthropic** (N.D. Cal., May 2025) | Real journal but **AI-invented title/authors** — "a world of difference between a missed citation and a hallucination." | Paragraph **struck.** |
| **Matter of Weber** (N.Y. Sur. Ct., 2024) | Expert used a chatbot to **"check" valuation math**, couldn't recall prompts or explain method. | Valuation **unreliable.** |
| **Ferlito v. Harbor Freight** (E.D.N.Y., Apr 2025) | **Safe harbor:** expert drafted independently, used AI only to **confirm** conclusions already reached. | Testimony **allowed.** |
| **Conservation Law Foundation v. Shell Oil** (D. Conn.) | Magistrate: AI **prompts are discoverable Rule-26 methodology.** | **NON-FINAL** — objected under Rule 72(a), stayed, **may change.** Always say so. |

> **The contrast that *is* the pitch:** the experts who got hurt let a tool
> **originate** something they couldn't account for; the one who was fine kept his
> **own judgment in the chair** and used AI only to confirm. Background: **FRE 702**
> (amended Dec 1 2023) + **ABA Formal Op. 512** (AI permitted with competence +
> disclosure). Lead with permission + the fix, never the threat.

## Glossary — ~20 terms you'll hear (plain English + expansion)

| Term | Expansion | One-line meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RAPEL** | Rehabilitation plan, Access, Placeability, Earning capacity, Labor force participation | The dominant peer-reviewed framework (Weed) for earning-capacity opinions; courts treat it as a recognized method. |
| **TSA** | Transferable Skills Analysis | Breaks down past work into skills, then finds which other occupations those skills open up; the report's evidentiary spine. |
| **DOT** | Dictionary of Occupational Titles | The classic occupational coding system; the forensic lingua franca for worker-trait detail. |
| **O\*NET** | Occupational Information Network | The modern U.S. DOL occupational database; reached from DOT via the **O\*NET–DOT crosswalk** for updated content. |
| **SVP** | Specific Vocational Preparation | How long it takes to learn a job; rule of thumb — **below SVP 3 generally isn't "transferable."** |
| **GED** | General Educational Development | **Not** the diploma — a Reasoning/Math/Language scale for a job's general demands; a match fails if levels are **exceeded.** |
| **MPSMS** | Materials, Products, Subject matter, Services | Codes for the *content* of the work (what's worked on / produced); used to find related occupations. |
| **OASYS / SkillTRAN / MVQS** | (product names) | The standard software engines that run TSA matching. They output data/tables — they **don't** write the narrative or pick the realistic occupations. |
| **Labor-market survey** | — | Real-world sampling of local employers for hireability and wages; where **placeability** is proven. The "equal distribution method" is a discredited shortcut (*Hohman v. Kijakazi*). |
| **Placeability** | — | Whether the person would actually get *hired* (vs. merely *able* to do the job) given restrictions, licensing, local economy. |
| **Earning capacity** | — | The *ability* to earn (a range), distinct from what was actually earned. |
| **Earning capacity vs. lost earnings** | — | **Capacity** = potential ability to earn going forward; **lost earnings** = concrete wages actually lost (often past). Capacity is the vocational expert's lane. |
| **Work-life expectancy** | — | How many more years of working life the person realistically has; reductions feed the loss calculation. |
| **Residual functional capacity (RFC)** | — | What the person can **still** do physically/cognitively after the injury — the driving assumption the whole opinion rests on. |
| **FCE** | Functional Capacity Evaluation | A clinical test measuring what the person can physically do; a preferred *source* for restrictions (vs. self-report). |
| **Vocational evaluation** | — | The assessment process — interview, testing, records review — that produces the vocational profile. |
| **Vocational expert (VE)** | — | The expert who renders the earning-capacity/employability opinion (CRC/ABVE). |
| **Transferability** | — | Whether skills from past work carry over to other occupations; the core question the TSA answers. |
| **Daubert** | (from *Daubert v. Merrell Dow*) | The judicial framework for screening expert reliability before an opinion is admitted. |
| **FRE 702** | Federal Rule of Evidence 702 | The gatekeeping rule (amended Dec 1 2023): sufficient facts/data + reliable application; **burden on the proponent.** |
| **Rule 26(a)(2)(B)** | Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2)(B) | The rule listing the 6 required contents of a retained expert's written report. |
| **Life-care planner (CLCP)** | Certified Life Care Planner (via ICHCC) | Builds the **future-cost-of-care** plan (a **different** deliverable from earning capacity). |
| **Forensic economist** | — | Takes the vocational expert's earning-capacity range and does the **present-value money math** over the work-life. |

## 8–10 phrases that signal you understand the field

1. "The TSA is really the **evidentiary spine** — it's what an opponent attacks first."
2. "Earning capacity should be a **range**, not a single number — a point figure invites a false-precision cross."
3. "The whole report lives or dies on whether the **data-to-opinion path is visible** — no unsupported leaps."
4. "Below **SVP 3**, skills generally aren't treated as transferable, right?"
5. "**Placeability** is the gap between *could do the job* and *would get hired* — that's where the labor-market survey comes in."
6. "Are you adopting restrictions from the **FCE / IME / treating physician**, rather than leaning on self-report?"
7. "RAPEL is **peer-reviewed**, so the Daubert attack usually isn't 'RAPEL is junk' — it's 'you didn't *do* a step rigorously for this person.'"
8. "Where does the **economist hand-off** sit — do you stop at the range and let them do present value?"
9. "The tool **structures your own findings** and records how AI was used. Its prompt is restricted to supplied evidence, source IDs are checked, and you verify, prepare, adopt, and sign."
10. "On **CLF v. Shell** — I know it's a **non-final magistrate order under Rule 72(a) review**, so a signal of direction, not settled law."

## 5 tells that mark you as a poser — avoid

1. ❌ Saying the tool **"drafts," "generates," or "writes" the report / findings.** *(Banned verbs — and to this audience, the exact thing that gets experts struck. Say "structures / organizes / formats your own findings.")*
2. ❌ Calling anything **"court-defensible," "admissible," or "Rule 26-compliant."** *(Admissibility is the **court's** call. Say "Rule 26(a)(2)(B)-structured / designed to support disclosure.")*
3. ❌ Confusing **GED** the developmental scale with the high-school equivalency **diploma** — or mixing up **earning capacity** with **lost earnings.** *(Basic vocabulary slips an expert catches instantly.)*
4. ❌ Conflating the **vocational expert** with the **life-care planner** or **forensic economist** — treating them as one role. *(They're three distinct deliverables.)*
5. ❌ Implying you've **written reports, testified, or worked with many experts** when you haven't — or presenting the **draft template** as the established standard. *(One faked credential or war story ends the trust in a referral community.)*
