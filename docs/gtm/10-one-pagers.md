## (A) FOR THE EXPERT

**[SECTION 1 — THE PROBLEM]**

### The question you now have to be ready for

Courts in 2025 and 2026 have struck expert declarations — not because the expert
used AI, but because the tool introduced something the expert couldn't verify,
or the expert couldn't reconstruct what they gave it and what came back.
*ABA Formal Opinion 512* says AI assistance is permitted with competence and
disclosure. The gap is the "disclosure" part: most experts working with AI tools
today don't have a methodology record they can produce if the question comes up.

*General information, not legal advice. Verify with counsel for your
jurisdiction.*

---

**[SECTION 2 — WHAT IT DOES]**

### Your findings become a structured, documented report

You supply your analysis — your notes, your data, the sources you relied on.
Disclosed. structures and assembles them into a Rule 26(a)(2)(B)-organized
report: opinions with basis and reasons, facts or data considered, exhibits,
qualifications, prior testimony, compensation statement.

By design, the tool cannot introduce a fact, figure, opinion, or citation you
did not supply. Every sentence must trace to a source you provided, or the tool
flags it and blocks export until you resolve it. You review the structured output,
independently verify it, edit it, adopt it, and sign it.

---

**[SECTION 3 — THE DISCLOSURE RECORD]**

### An AI-Disclosure Appendix built to produce, not reconstruct

Every AI-assisted section is logged automatically in a tamper-evident,
append-only record: which section, which model and version, and exactly the
evidence you gave it — and nothing you did not give it. That log becomes an
AI-Disclosure Appendix attached to your export.

If your AI use comes up in discovery or on cross-examination, you have a
contemporaneous methodology record ready to hand over. You don't reconstruct
it from memory months later.

The record is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: each entry is cryptographically
linked to the one before it, so a later edit or deletion is detectable.

If you used no AI on a section, the disclosure says so. There is also a fully
deterministic mode — no AI model involved at all — for reports where you want
no AI in the chain.

**[SECTION 4 — HOW IT STAYS HONEST]**

### Built so you stay the expert

- **Closed-world grounding.** The tool only works with what you gave it. Gap → flag, not fill-in.
- **Live grounding feedback.** As you type, each sentence shows whether it traces to a source you supplied.
- **Export blocked on gaps.** A single ungrounded sentence prevents export until you resolve it.
- **You author and sign every time.** The tool assembles; it does not author.
- **Confidentiality.** Case files are processed in your browser — the document never leaves your computer during intake. Encrypted in transit and at rest. We commit to not training on your data; a written zero-retention agreement with our model provider is in progress.

**[SECTION 2 — THE TOOL'S ROLE]**

### It organizes the expert's own findings — it does not originate them

The tool structures and formats material the expert supplies into the Rule
26(a)(2)(B) report format. By design it cannot introduce a fact, figure,
opinion, or citation the expert did not provide. Every factual sentence must
cite a source the expert supplied; anything that cannot be tied to a supplied
source is flagged, and export is blocked until the expert resolves it.

The expert independently verifies, reviews, adopts, and signs.
Authorship and liability sit with the expert, not with the tool.

---

**[SECTION 3 — THE DISCLOSURE RECORD]**

### Why the AI-Disclosure Appendix matters for discovery

Courts in 2025 and 2026 have begun examining how experts used AI tools in their
work. In *Conservation Law Foundation v. Shell Oil* (D. Conn.), a magistrate
ordered an expert to produce her AI prompts as discoverable methodology under
Rule 26. That order is non-final — objected to under Rule 72(a) and stayed
pending district-court review — but the direction is clear: how an expert
prompted an AI tool can be part of the methodology subject to discovery.

*General information, not legal advice. CLF v. Shell is a non-final order and
may change. Verify against the official reporter and consult counsel for your
jurisdiction.*

Disclosed. builds the methodology record in advance:

- **What is logged:** each AI-assisted section, the model and version, and the
  exact evidence the expert gave it — and nothing it was not given.
- **How it is kept:** append-only, tamper-evident (each entry cryptographically
  linked to the one before it; a later edit or deletion is detectable).
- **What it produces:** an AI-Disclosure Appendix in the exported report.

If your expert's AI use comes up in a motion to compel or on cross, they have
a contemporaneous, structured record to produce — not a reconstruction from
memory.

The Rule 26(a)(2)(B) structure the tool enforces also addresses the
"facts or data considered" and "basis and reasons" elements that cross-
examination most often targets.

**[SECTION 4 — ADMISSIBILITY AND LIMITS]**

### What the tool does not guarantee

Admissibility is the court's determination, not ours. The tool is designed to
support a methodology the expert can account for and to disclose AI use in the
format these cases are pointing toward; it does not and cannot guarantee that
a report or disclosure satisfies any particular court or jurisdiction.

Confidentiality: case files are processed in the expert's browser during intake
(the document does not leave the expert's machine at that step) and are
encrypted in transit and at rest. A written zero-retention agreement with the
model provider is in progress; we describe this as a commitment, not a completed
certification. The expert remains responsible for confirming that uploading any
given material is permitted under the applicable protective order.
