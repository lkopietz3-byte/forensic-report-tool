# Design-Partner & Discovery Kit

Everything you need to land **1–2 beachhead design partners** and run **5–10
discovery interviews** — the plan's single biggest "extremely hard without it"
item (Must-Have #2). The product is ready to show; this is the go-to-market
motion. Copy is ready to send. The hook throughout is the **sample report**
(`/sample`, or the PDF/DOCX export) and the **Resources article** on the recent
AI-disclosure rulings.

> **Honesty rules apply to every word of outreach** (see CLAUDE.md). Never say
> "court-defensible," "admissible," "compliant," or "guaranteed." Say the tool
> **structures/organizes your own findings**, restricts the model to supplied
> evidence, checks citation IDs, and produces a **tamper-evident AI-disclosure
> record**. The expert still verifies substantive support; admissibility is the
> court's call. Overclaiming to a skeptical expert is how you lose them.

---

## 1. The goal (and why now)

- **Primary:** convert one credible beachhead-discipline expert into a paid-or-free
  **design partner / advisor** before any further heavy build.
- **Secondary:** 5–10 discovery interviews to settle the open questions —
  report-writing pain, **volume per year**, willingness to pay, exact template
  expectations, and whether the **AI-disclosure layer is perceived as valuable**
  (the moat thesis).
- **Why experts will take the call now:** the AI-disclosure question just got real
  (*Kohls v. Ellison*, *Concord v. Anthropic*, *CLF v. Shell*). You're offering a
  conversation about a risk they're already worried about — not a sales pitch.

**Pick ONE beachhead discipline to start** (don't spread across all three). Order
of accessibility, per the plan: **forensic vocational rehabilitation** (the
template we've already drafted, RAPEL), then **accident reconstruction**
(IPTM/ACTAR), then **forensic engineering** (ASTM E3176).

---

## 2. The offer (the value exchange)

**What the design partner gets:**
- Free use of the product for their matters, for life (or through launch + 1 yr).
- Direct influence over their discipline's template — it gets tuned to *their*
  format and methodology.
- Founding-advisor recognition (if they want it) and first-mover standing in their
  referral community.
- A tool that reclaims the 3–5 hours/report they hate writing — built around *their*
  workflow.

**What you ask in return (keep it light):**
- 2–3 short calls over a few weeks.
- Red-team one real (de-identified) report end-to-end against their standard.
- Honest feedback, and — only if they're comfortable — one or two intros to peers.

**What you never ask:** their case data under protective order, money, or a public
endorsement before they believe in it.

## 4. Outreach copy — starting points, NOT scripts

> **The single biggest tell that a message is mass-mailed (or AI-written) is a
> generic opener and a too-polished, every-sentence-the-same-length body.** Don't
> send any of these word-for-word. Rewrite the first line so it's obviously about
> *them*, cut a sentence, and let it sound like you typed it on your phone. A short,
> slightly rough email from a real person beats a perfect one every time.

**Default ask = async, not a call.** Point people to the sample and the short
**application at `/for-experts`**. It asks three focused questions — time/pain,
their AI experience, and what it must (never) do to earn their trust — so you get
real signal, not one vague paragraph. It&apos;s **gated for quality**: blank or
one-word submissions are turned away, and a free slot is **earned, not
auto-granted** — you review the applications and offer slots to the good ones.
Save calls for the warm ones.

### Cold email — A (the time sink)
*Personalize the first line — reference a case, a talk they gave, their directory
listing, a mutual contact. Then:*

> Hi [Name] — quick one, and there's nothing to buy.
>
> I'm building a tool for [vocational] experts that does the report-writing grind
> for you without putting words in your mouth. It takes your own findings, puts them
> in Rule 26 format, and flat refuses to add a fact or citation you didn't give it.
> It also keeps a record of how AI was used, so you've got a clean answer when
> someone asks.
>
> I'd rather hear from people who actually write these than keep guessing. Here's a
> 2-minute sample: [/sample]. If it's any good, there's a short form to tell me
> what's missing: [/for-experts]. Either way, even a one-line reply helps.
>
> [Your name]

### Cold email — B (the AI question)
> Hi [Name] — "did you use AI, and how?" is turning into a deposition question. An
> expert's declaration got tossed in *Kohls v. Ellison* over made-up cites, and in
> *CLF v. Shell* a court told an expert to hand over her AI prompts.
>
> I wrote up where that leaves experts: [/resources/ai-disclosure-in-expert-reports].
> I'm also building the thing that fixes it — your findings, your report, an
> automatic record of the AI use, you as the author. Worth a look? Sample's here
> [/sample], and there's a 5-min form if you want to weigh in: [/for-experts].

### LinkedIn note (keep it under ~300 chars, no link if LinkedIn strips it)
> Hi [Name] — building a report + AI-disclosure tool for [vocational] experts. You
> stay the author; it won't invent opinions or cites. Not selling anything, just want
> a few experts to tell me what's wrong with it. Mind if I send a 2-min sample?

### Warm intro (forward this to a mutual contact)
> Do you know any [vocational / accident-recon / forensic] expert witnesses? I'm
> building a report tool for them and want one or two to shape it — free, for good.
> Happy to send a 30-second sample if anyone comes to mind.

### Make it sound like you, not a robot — delete these tells
- Generic openers: "I hope this email finds you well," "I'm reaching out because."
- Throat-clearing: "In today's evolving landscape," "As you may know."
- Buzzwords: leverage, robust, seamless, streamline, cutting-edge, delve, synergy.
- Tricolons and perfectly parallel clauses in every sentence (the AI cadence).
- Em-dash overload. Mix in short fragments. Vary sentence length on purpose.
- Three exclamation points of enthusiasm. One real, specific detail beats all of it.
- A wall of text. If it's longer than your phone screen, cut it.

## 5. Discovery interview script (~20 minutes) — for the warm follow-ups

You don't need this for everyone. The **`/for-experts` form does the first pass
async** — it already captures discipline, **report volume**, and the open-ended
pain/needs answer. Use this script only for the people whose form answers (or
reply) look promising and who are open to a quick call.

**Rule #1: listen, don't pitch.** You're validating, not selling. Ask, then shut up.
**Rule #2: never lead the witness** — don't ask "wouldn't an AI-disclosure record be
great?" Ask open questions and let them volunteer it.

**Open (2 min).** "Thanks for the time. I'm trying to learn how report writing
actually works for you before I build the wrong thing — mind if I mostly ask
questions?"

**The work (6 min).**
1. Walk me through your last report start to finish — where does the time go?
2. Which parts do you dread or write off as unbillable?
3. How many reports do you write in a typical year? *(settles per-report vs. seat
   pricing — Fatal Risk #4)*
4. What format/standard do you work to? *(RAPEL? ASTM E3176? IPTM topical?)* What do
   reviewers/opposing counsel nitpick most?

**AI + disclosure (5 min) — don't lead.**
5. Are you using any AI in your practice today? What for? What worries you about it?
6. *(If not raised)* Has the AI-disclosure question come up in your matters or your
   association? *(Listen for whether the moat resonates unprompted.)*
7. If a judge or opposing counsel asked how you used AI, how would you answer today?

**Money (3 min).**
8. If a tool reclaimed [X hours] per report with you still as the author, what would
   that be worth? Per report, or a subscription?
9. What would make you *not* trust a tool like this? *(Find the deal-breakers.)*

**Close (3 min).**
10. Show the **sample** (or send before the call). "What's wrong with this? What
    would your version need?"
11. The ask: "I'm taking on two or three experts to shape the [discipline] template —
    free, for as long as you'll have it. Would you want one of those slots?" Or, if
    not a fit: "Who else writes these well that I should learn from?"

**After every call:** write down (a) the time/pain in their words, (b) volume/year,
(c) the willingness-to-pay number, (d) whether AI-disclosure mattered *unprompted*,
(e) the #1 objection. These five answers are the validation.

## 6. Lightweight design-partner terms

Keep it a one-paragraph understanding, not a contract:
- **Access is granted on acceptance.** The `/for-experts` form is an application,
  not an automatic account — you review it, and accepted partners get an invite
  plus early access when their discipline is ready. Worthy of a slot ≈ a practicing
  expert in a beachhead discipline, writing reports regularly, who gave thoughtful
  answers (not a blank form).
- They get free access [scope/duration]; they help shape the [discipline] template
  and red-team output.
- **Professional responsibility for any real report stays with them** — the tool
  structures their findings, restricts model output to supplied evidence, and
  checks citation IDs; they independently verify, prepare, adopt, and sign.
- You won't use their case data to train models; case files stay confidential.
- Either side can walk away anytime; nothing is published about them without consent.

## 7. Pipeline & cadence

Track a simple funnel toward a target (e.g., **40 contacted → 10 calls → 1–2
partners**):

| Stage | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Identified | 50 |
| Contacted | 40 |
| Replied | ~12 |
| Call booked | ~10 |
| Call done | 8–10 |
| Design partner / advisor | 1–2 |

**Weekly cadence:** 10–15 new contacts, follow up twice (day 4, day 10), run every
call that books, log the five answers, and re-tune the template + pitch from what you
hear. Two good design partners beat fifty cold emails — concentrate.

---

## 8. Do / Don't

- **Do:** lead with their pain and the legal moment; send the sample early; ask open
  questions; offer real value (free access, template influence); keep every claim
  honest; convert one interviewee into a paid/free design partner before more build.
- **Don't:** pitch on the discovery call; lead the witness on the moat; overclaim
  admissibility or certifications; ask for case data; spread across three disciplines
  at once; build the next feature before you've talked to ten experts.
