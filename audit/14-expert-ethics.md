# Audit 14 — Professional Ethics & Target-Market Trust Positioning

**Scope:** Does the product's framing protect the expert from an ethics/credibility attack on cross-examination, or does copy/UX create ammunition for opposing counsel? Covers ABVE, CRC/CRCC, and IARP codes; general expert-witness norms; the permissible drafting-assistance vs. impermissible opinion-origination line; ghostwriting; and deposition-safe positioning.

---

## Issues

### [HIGH] "We draft from your findings" — the verb "draft" is double-edged
**File:** `src/app/page.tsx`, Step 02 heading and hero `<h1>`  
**Problem:** "We draft from your findings" and "drafted from your findings" will read — to opposing counsel — as "the AI wrote your report." Cross-exam question: "Sir, didn't you just pay a service to draft this report for you?" The word "draft" does not carry the nuance that the product only re-formats the expert's own stated conclusions; it implies origination. ABVE Canon 7 (independent opinion), CRCC Code Section H (competence/integrity of opinions), and IARP Standards of Practice all require that the expert's opinions be the product of the expert's own professional judgment. Any framing that suggests the software "drafted" the substance can be cited by opposing counsel as evidence the expert outsourced their judgment.  
**Fix:** Replace "We draft from your findings" with **"We structure your findings into report format"** or **"We format your findings — nothing else."** In the hero `<h1>`, replace "drafted from your findings" with **"structured from your findings"** or **"organized from your own findings."** The word "structure/format/organize" is what the product actually does and is exactly parallel to what a legal secretary or paralegal does — a comparison experts already accept as proper delegation (see ABA Formal Op. 08-451 on ghostwriting; ABVE does not prohibit clerical structuring assistance).

### [HIGH] DISCLOSURE_STATEMENT says "reviewed, verified, and adopted" — but that needs to be true
**File:** `src/lib/domain/disclosure.ts` (`DISCLOSURE_STATEMENT` constant)  
**Problem:** The appendix states "The expert reviewed, verified, and adopted all content." This is an assertion of fact that will be tested under oath. If the export flow does not require the expert to *explicitly* approve each section (section-by-section), opposing counsel can argue the statement is false or a pro-forma checkbox. The current `Workspace.tsx` does have a per-section "Approve section" gate — but approval only requires the section to be "clean" (no ungrounded sentences), not that the expert read it. There is no timestamp per approval, no audit-log entry per section approval, and no confirmation that the approval was a human act (vs. a bot clicking through the demo).  
**Fix:** (a) Log a timestamped `APPROVED_BY_EXPERT` event into the audit trail for each section, keyed to the expert's authenticated session. (b) Before export, surface a mandatory declaration screen: **"I, [Expert Name], certify that I have read, understood, and verified the accuracy of each section above, and that the opinions expressed are my own professional judgments."** Add an explicit checkbox or typed-signature step, not just a button click. (c) Include that per-section timestamp log in the appendix table so it can be produced on discovery.

### [HIGH] The "AI-Disclosure Appendix" is a double-edged asset without a framing paragraph
**File:** `src/app/page.tsx` disclosure section, `src/lib/domain/disclosure.ts`  
**Problem:** The appendix is marketed as protection ("walk in already disclosed"), and it is — but in the wrong hands it hands opposing counsel a roadmap. The appendix currently discloses model, version, and which evidence was fed per section. It does NOT currently include: (a) that the expert's opinions were provided by the expert as inputs (not generated by the AI); (b) that the AI output was constrained to restatement and formatting; (c) a description of the review/approval step. Without those three elements, opposing counsel reads the appendix as "here is the list of sections the AI wrote, here is what it was fed." The appendix needs a header paragraph that affirmatively frames what the AI did and did not do — in court-ready language — not just a table.  
**Fix:** Add to the top of the appendix, before the table: **"[Expert name]'s opinions, conclusions, and supporting analysis are [expert name]'s own professional judgments, formed independently before use of this tool. The tool's function was limited to (1) organizing and formatting the expert's stated findings into a standard report structure and (2) confirming that every factual sentence cites a source provided by the expert. The tool did not generate, suggest, or modify any opinion or conclusion. The following table discloses, by section, the model version used, the evidence it was supplied, and the date and time the expert reviewed and approved each section."**  
This matches the protective framing that courts applying Daubert and courts issuing AI-disclosure orders (e.g., CLF v. Shell, D. Conn. 2026) expect to see.

### [MEDIUM] "You are the author. Approve to lock this section for export." — gatekeeping copy is right but incomplete
**File:** `src/app/app/Workspace.tsx`, DraftStage footer text  
**Problem:** The copy is correct in principle, but "lock this section for export" frames approval as a mechanical export step rather than a substantive professional act. Under ABVE Canon 6 (responsible use of data and methodology) and CRCC Section H.1 (objectivity), an expert who testifies that they "clicked approve to unlock export" will be far more vulnerable than one who testifies "I certified in writing that I had independently reviewed and agreed with each section." The distinction is whether the approval was a substantive act or a UI gate.  
**Fix:** Change the footer text to: **"You certify that this section reflects your own professional findings and opinions. Approving records that certification."** The button label should change from "Approve section" to **"Certify this section."** A tooltip or modal can note: "This adds a timestamped certification to the audit log under your name."

### [MEDIUM] Hero/guardrails claim "cannot add a fact … from its own knowledge" — this is an architectural promise, not a verified constraint
**File:** `src/app/page.tsx`, GUARDRAILS — "Never originates facts or opinions"  
**Problem:** The system prompt in `src/lib/draft/prompts.ts` does instruct the model to use only supplied evidence, and the grounding checker (`checkGrounding`) flags ungrounded sentences. But the claim "It cannot add a fact" is technically overclaimed: a language model CAN add facts; the product's architecture attempts to prevent it via prompting and post-hoc grounding checks, but hallucination is not architecturally impossible. If an expert is cross-examined on this and the opposing expert demonstrates a hallucination, the marketing claim becomes evidence of recklessness.  
**Fix:** Soften to: **"Constrained to use only the evidence you supply — any sentence not tied to your evidence is flagged and blocked from export."** This is still a strong, accurate claim that reflects what the system actually does (prompt constraint + grounding block on export) without an absolute technical guarantee that could be falsified.

### [MEDIUM] "Ghostwriting" framing gap — the product does not preemptively address the ABVE/CRC ghostwriting question
**File:** Marketing copy generally; no explicit treatment anywhere  
**Problem:** ABVE Canon 1 and CRCC Code Section A.1 (integrity) are read by some practitioners and ethics committees as requiring that the expert personally write the substance of their report, not merely sign it. The analogy to staff drafting assistance is the correct and professionally accepted counter-argument — but the product never makes it. Competing copy on the web (T.C. Kelly, ExpertPages, cited in market research) says "Using an AI program to generate an expert report that the expert claims to have authored would be unethical." That framing is wrong about how Disclosed. works, but it will be the first objection a skeptical expert raises. There is no page, FAQ, or in-product framing that directly addresses it.  
**Fix:** Add a short "Ethics & professional responsibility" FAQ section (or landing-page callout) that explicitly addresses the ghostwriting question with the correct analogy: **"Experts have always been permitted to use staff, paralegals, and consultants to structure and format their reports — what the codes prohibit is delegating the formation of the expert's opinions. Disclosed. does only what a skilled paralegal does: it formats what you tell it, organized around the sources you supply. Your opinions are entered by you; no opinion is generated by the tool. This is the same permissible assistance that every professional organization's ethics commentary has consistently upheld."** Cite ABVE Canon 7 footnote, CRCC Code Section H, and the SEAK expert-witness practice norms to give it authority.

### [LOW] "Authored by you · no AI" badge on profile sections is the only place the product distinguishes AI-assisted vs. non-AI-assisted content
**File:** `src/app/app/Workspace.tsx`, DraftStage — profile section badge  
**Problem:** The badge is correct and useful, but it only appears for profile sections. A cross-examining attorney may ask: "Which parts of this report did the AI help write?" The current disclosure appendix answers this by listing assisted sections. But there is no corresponding in-product visual that makes the same distinction visible to the expert during drafting, so the expert cannot easily rehearse or verify the answer. This is a trust/comfort gap, not a legal one.  
**Fix:** At export, include a one-paragraph plain-English summary: **"Sections drafted with AI formatting assistance: [list]. Sections entered directly by the expert without AI: [list]."** This mirrors the appendix but gives the expert an easy mental map of what they need to be ready to defend.

### [LOW] The DISCLOSURE_STATEMENT boilerplate uses passive voice and no expert name — feels unattributed
**File:** `src/lib/domain/disclosure.ts`  
**Problem:** "The expert reviewed, verified, and adopted all content" is third-person and could be read as a tool-generated assertion rather than the expert's first-person certification. If the appendix is produced in discovery, a savvy opposing attorney will note that the expert never actually signed or adopted the statement.  
**Fix:** Generate the statement in first-person, attributed to the named expert and dated: **"I, [Expert Name], [CRC/CVE/ABVE-D], certify that the opinions expressed in this report are my own professional judgments. I used Disclosed. solely as a drafting-assistance tool to organize and format findings I supplied. I reviewed and certified each section on [date]. I did not instruct the tool to form, generate, or modify any opinion."** This converts a tool-generated boilerplate into a first-person expert certification, which is what a court or opposing counsel actually needs to see.

## Deposition-Safe Description

The following is a sentence the expert could say under oath in response to "Who actually wrote this report?" or "Did a computer form your opinions?":

> "I used a report-formatting tool called Disclosed. that I'm familiar with. I supplied all of my findings, data, and opinions to the tool. The tool organized and formatted that material into a standard report structure and flagged any sentence that wasn't tied to a source I had provided. No opinion or conclusion in this report was generated, suggested, or modified by the software — those are my own professional judgments. I reviewed and certified each section individually before the report was finalized, and the disclosure appendix attached to this report records exactly which sections were formatted with that assistance, the software version, and the date I approved each one."

This answer is accurate under the current architecture, distinguishes permissible formatting assistance from impermissible opinion origination, maps directly to the ABVE/CRCC permissible-assistance standard, and preemptively addresses the ghostwriting angle.

## Top 3 Must-Fix

1. **Replace "We draft from your findings" / "drafted from your findings" with "structure/format/organize" language** — across `page.tsx` Step 02, hero `<h1>`, and any other surface. "Draft" is the single word most likely to be weaponized on cross-examination. (HIGH, one-hour copy change.)

2. **Convert the disclosure appendix statement to a first-person expert certification with per-section timestamps** — `disclosure.ts` DISCLOSURE_STATEMENT, the export flow in `Workspace.tsx`, and the appendix table. Without a named, dated, first-person certification, the appendix is a tool-generated claim, not an expert declaration. (HIGH, requires audit-log + UI work.)

3. **Add an explicit Ethics & professional responsibility FAQ addressing the ghostwriting/ABVE question** — with the correct "paralegal structuring" analogy and citations to ABVE Canon 7 / CRCC Section H. This is the #1 adoption blocker in voice-of-customer research (market-research.md §7) and is currently completely unaddressed in the product copy. (HIGH, marketing/copy, no code changes required.)
